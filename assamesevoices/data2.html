Welcome to Assamese Voices

Hello!

We are Saurabh and Kaushika from BhaxaLab. We are linguists, currently based at the Australian National University and Delhi University, and our work centres on Assamese language and linguistics.

So, have you ever wondered what Assamese sounded like 50 years ago? Or 100 years ago? Would it have sounded the same as the Assamese we speak today? You may also have heard parents say that their children speak differently from them, or that younger people no longer speak Assamese in quite the same way. Or you have almost certainly heard people say that someone from Barpeta sounds different from someone from Jorhat or Sivasagar. We all have observations like these about the way Assamese is spoken.

This is exactly what interests us. We want to understand how present-day Assamese is similar or different across speakers, and how the way people speak may vary across places, age groups, genders, and other factors. To study this, we need audio recordings of how people actually speak. There is, of course, plenty of Assamese audio and video material available today, but only a small amount of it is suitable for the kind of linguistic research we do.

So, as a first step, we have designed this pilot project to collect short audio recordings from Assamese speakers. The task is simple and should take no more than 10 minutes to complete. If you have any questions, feedback, or simply something you would like to share with us about Assamese, we would be very happy to hear from you.

Thank you for your interest in participating!

Before you start

A few recording tips

  • Find a quiet place with as little background noise as possible.
  • Hold your phone naturally, as you normally would while using or typing on it.
  • Speak naturally, the way you normally would speak with family at home.
  • Say only the word shown on the screen. The illustration is there to help if the word is unfamiliar.
  • You can listen to your recording and re-record it if needed.
  • Please try to complete all the recordings in one sitting.
